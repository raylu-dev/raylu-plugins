# Travel Meetings

Answer "who should I meet while I'm in [city]?" from the user's own companies in Raylu, using Raylu's location data rather than the CRM's. Company names come from a market map or a list the user already has. Location comes from Raylu: company HQ, or where the CEO actually lives (from LinkedIn), which is often a different city.

## When to use

- The user names a city and wants meetings from companies they already track.
- Not for finding new companies in a city. That is a market map. If they want new targets, say so and offer to start one.

## Flow

Ask one question per message. Skip any the user already answered. If everything was answered in the first message, send one short restatement of the assumptions and start.

1. **City and dates.** Usually given. A region name (Bay Area, DFW, Tri-State) is not a city: use its principal city as the centre, say so, and invite a correction. Do not ask for dates up front; if you need a month for naming, infer it ("the 20th" means the next 20th) and say "roughly". Dates are asked only at step 9, and only if outreach is generated.
2. **Where should I look?** "Point me at a market map or a list (e.g. your CRM lists)." Resolve names with `search_lists_and_maps(search: <the distinctive word from the user>)`; it labels each match List, CRM list, or Market map, so one call covers both kinds. Exact match wins; mention near-misses in one clause. No exact match and more than one near-miss: ask which, and say which one you would guess. No name from the user: show the lists whose names suggest a pipeline (Pipeline, Priority, Portfolio, Callback) and, for a customer with a CRM integration, the CRM-owned-accounts route from Fund-specific names. When several lists share one name, never pick by name; use an ID from the user. When the customer's maps carry `isInCRM`, ask here, as part of the source question: "Only companies already in your CRM, or everyone on the map?" Read the company count from the tool's own count field, never from a number inside the list's description; the size gate uses it.
3. **How wide a net?** "How far are you willing to drive? Any number works; people usually say somewhere between 25 and 100 miles." Use the figure and the unit they give, miles or kilometres, throughout.
4. **Which location?** Offer both, recommend one:
   - "CEO location: where the CEO actually lives, from LinkedIn. I recommend this if you want to meet the CEO."
   - "Company HQ: I recommend this if any executive is fine."
   - If they cannot choose, treat it as CEO location. On a market map both come back together anyway; the choice only sets which column leads.
5. **Metro set.** Now that you have the radius, build the set of place names inside it from your own geography: the city, its boroughs or districts, its suburbs, neighbouring towns. Measure straight-line, centre to centre; the distance band is the one judgment in the table that comes from you, not a tool, so state the set in the output (one line per band, or counties and regions with named exceptions for a large metro) so the user can correct it. "In city" means the city and its boroughs or districts (the five boroughs for New York, the London boroughs for London). Match on city plus region together: the state for US trips; for other countries, any sub-national label of that country (home nation, county, borough, district, postcode fragment), since the data's region column holds whichever it has. A row whose city slot holds a street, a county, or a region name has an unknown city and a known region. A city name shared across countries (London, Cambridge, Perth, Richmond, Windsor, Kingston) never matches on city alone; a blank region with a shared name is unknown. A city string that begins with the city name matches ("London Ec V Nx", "London Sw Bz" are London). A region value that is a borough of the trip city ("Lambeth", "Westminster", "Sutton") counts as in city; say so. A returned city you cannot place ("Glenview, New York") goes to "Checked, not in range" with a flag and still counts as checked. For up to five rows with a blank region and a name that could be inside the radius, spend one `get_companies` call to resolve them. A list name and a resolved company name that disagree, or a domain the tool remapped, get one clause; that comes from the tool, not from memory.
6. **Run** the matching path. Say what you are doing and roughly how long.
7. **Deliver** with the output contract.
8. **Contacts?** After the list: "Want email and phone for these?" (or "for [Company]?" when there is one match). On a yes, use any Contacts row already returned by `get_companies` that is not the user themself; that is a vendor contact record and already counts as a contact, though a row with no title does not identify who the CEO is. Run `find_company_contact` only for companies with no such row. When the contact record names a CEO who differs from the CEO the map or people search showed, the map's record is probably stale: the map and people search come from scraped LinkedIn titles, while contact records come from vendors that track the current officeholder. Say so. If the company matched on CEO location, re-run `company_people_search(company)` for the new name. In range: keep the CEO match. Out of range or not found: drop the CEO leg only; the company stays if its HQ is in range, otherwise it moves to "Checked, not in range" with the reason. Then ask which person the outreach should go to; if the user says "the CEO", use the contact record's name and say so.
9. **Actions.** Offer, do not do: save as a list named "[City] trip [Month Year]"; generate campaigns with a "City Visit" cadence. Only when the user says yes, call `list_strategies(includeTemplates: true)`. If the user already owns a copy such as "City Visit (Copy)", use it; otherwise copy the template with `copy_cadence` first, since templates cannot be generated from directly. Ask for the exact trip days if you do not have them; "the week of Oct 20" is not enough. Always pass `contactEmail` (the person chosen at step 8) to `generate_campaign`; without it the campaign goes to the company's primary contact, which in some projects is the user's own CRM record. `generate_campaign` cannot fill the template's custom fields (trip city, trip dates), so each draft comes back in "needs attention". Clear it yourself: confirm generation with `get_run_status(feature: "company", query: <domain>, type: "outreach")`, find the campaign ID with an unfiltered `list_campaigns` (the name search misses new campaigns), call `get_campaign(campaignId)`, and for every email step whose subject or body still contains `[Trip city]` or `[Trip dates]`, call `update_email(emailId, subject, bodyText)` with the city and dates written in. Leave `[Your Name]`; it is a system variable filled at send. Read the campaign back and confirm no custom-field brackets remain in any email step. The LinkedIn step's note cannot be read or edited through the connector and its prompt also names the trip fields, so tell the user to check that one step in the app. The campaign keeps showing "needs attention" until someone opens it in the app; say so. Generate as drafts, never auto-start. If the user did not give dates, leave the drafts as they are and tell them the two values to type in the Campaigns tab. There is no email notification in Raylu; do not offer one.

## Path A: market map (two calls, complete)

`get_run_status(feature: "market_map", query)` then `get_list_or_map_rows(map: query, columns: ["name","domain","city","state","ceoName","ceoLocation","isInCRM"])`, asking for the next `page` while the first line says more remain. Every row, HQ and CEO location together. Always show both. The map's `state` column holds whatever sub-national label the data has: a US state, a country, a county, or a borough. If the row count is a few short of the map's listed count, report the gap in the coverage line; do not retry. `isInCRM` comes back as the strings "Yes, in CRM" or "No, not in CRM"; when the user chose the in-CRM filter, rows flagged No are neither matches nor "not in range", so give their count in one clause and leave them out of every table. Count in-range companies after that filter. When ten or fewer are in range and any lacks a CEO on the map, run the Path B people search for those, so a CEO-led trip has names; when more than ten are in range and some lack a CEO, offer the same capped pull Path B offers. CEO names arrive as scraped LinkedIn strings with credentials ("FRCS Dr. Jane Doe MDCM"); keep the honorific and name, drop the letters. The run-status tool's own description says to call it only on request; the map-data tool asks for it first, so one call is right.

Reading `ceoLocation`: a city string matches on city and region; any string that names the trip's metro ("San Francisco Bay Area", "Greater Seattle Area", "Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex", "Greater London", "London Area, United Kingdom", "Greater Paris Metropolitan Region", "Zürich Metropolitan Area", "Metro Vancouver") counts as matched with band "metro, city not given"; a country-only or blank string is unknown, never "not there"; when the country named is not the trip's country, write "unknown, different country".

## Path B: list (paged, expensive)

1. **Size gate first**, from the count the list tool returned. Up to 200 companies: continue. More than 200: stop and say: "This list has N companies. Resolving HQ costs about two lookups per 50 companies with large results, and CEO location costs one or two lookups per company, so I can do about 200 in one pass on HQ and about 40 on CEO. Options: point me at a smaller list or a market map, or I run the first 200 and you tell me whether to keep going." Wait for the answer. Never pick names that sound local and check only those.
2. `get_list_or_map_rows(list: query, columns: ["name", "domain"], page: n)` for each page. Names and domains only; pass domains through as they come, even with a path.
3. HQ: `get_companies(queries: [up to 50 domains])` per batch. Each batch is large and may be saved to a file rather than shown; read the file and take the Location line and any Contacts rows for each company. A "[Preview]" tag means an abbreviated record; its Location is still usable. Do not take location or the CEO name from the Contacts block here; a row that is the user themself is their own CRM record, and vendor rows are used only at step 8.
4. CEO, when the location choice is CEO: `company_people_search(company, seniority: "Founder")`. If no row has a CEO title, including when zero people come back, call again with `seniority: "C-Level"`. Those two values are the ones the data uses; "C-Suite" is a department, not a seniority. One or two calls per company. Up to 40 companies: run it for all of them. Above 40: run it for companies whose HQ is in the trip's region or an adjacent one, up to 40 companies, and list the companies skipped. People-search locations come back as "City, Country" with no state; an ambiguous city name ("Lewisville, United States") stays uncounted and is flagged, since no tool resolves it. For an in-range company, always run both calls. Zero people in both calls means unknown. People returned but none titled CEO also means the CEO is unknown: write "unknown (no CEO title returned)" in the CEO column, and any of those executives who live in range get an "other exec" row.
5. When the location choice is HQ and there are ten or fewer in-range companies, run the CEO lookup above for those companies anyway, so the table has names to meet. When more than ten are in range, the table has no names; say so and offer a capped CEO pull: "I can look up who runs up to 40 of these. Which ones, or shall I take the first 40?"

## Output contract

1. One line: list or map name, radius and centre city, location field, how many companies were checked, and the words "location only, not ranked". Then the place set used for the bands.
2. Table of matches only, sorted by distance band, with columns: Company, HQ city, CEO name, CEO city, Matched on (HQ / CEO / both / other exec), Distance band. Bands use the user's unit and radius: "in city", "within half of it" ("within 15 mi" for 30 miles, "within 40 km" for 80 km), "within [radius]", and "metro, city not given". When HQ and CEO land in different bands, the band column shows the one for the location field the user chose, and the other appears in its own column's text. A co-founder, chair, or other executive who lives in range gets their own row with "other exec" and their title, since the user may still want that meeting. When CEO data was not pulled, the CEO columns say "not pulled". More than 40 matches: give counts per band and a compact name list per band instead of one row each.
3. A separate short table titled "CEO nearby, HQ elsewhere" when the CEO path found any. These are the ones the CRM would have missed.
4. "Just outside the radius", with the outer bound stated ("40 to 60 mi"): companies within roughly half the radius beyond it, one line each, so a boundary call is the user's.
5. "Checked, not in range": the remaining companies with their HQ city, so the user can see the whole list was covered. Up to 50 companies: list them all. More than 50: list only those in the trip's region (or with a CEO in the trip's region) and any whose name suggests the trip's country but whose data places them elsewhere, and give the count of the rest in one clause.
6. One line on coverage: rows read versus the map or list count, how many had no HQ, how many had no CEO data, and any skipped by the CEO cap.
7. The contacts question, then the actions offer.

Everything in the table comes from a Raylu tool result, except the distance band, which is stated as your estimate. No company is placed in a city from memory, and no "they have a Dallas office" from general knowledge. If a fact did not come back from a tool, it is not in the table.

## Common mistakes

- Bundling the three questions into one message.
- Asking about sector, goal, or check size. Not this skill's job.
- Resolving a list name from the first ten rows only.
- Paging through a big list before applying the size gate.
- Guessing which names on a big list are local instead of applying the size gate.
- Matching "London" without checking the country.
- Ranking or annotating companies from outside knowledge. The table is location data only.
- Treating a blank CEO location or an empty people search as "not in the city".
- Putting the Contacts block from `get_companies` in the CEO column.
- Offering an email digest. There is no tool for it.
